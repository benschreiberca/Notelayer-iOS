# MARKETING 03: Official Notelayer Twitter / X Page

Last Updated: 2026-04-20
Status: Draft
Type: Marketing Plan
Channel: Twitter / X
Priority: Medium

## Purpose

Establish an official @Notelayer (or closest available handle) Twitter / X account to give the product a public presence, point traffic to the website and App Store, and build an audience separately from the founder's personal account.

## Why a Product Account (Not Just Personal)

- A product account can be handed off or co-managed.
- It keeps the product's identity separate from personal opinions.
- It serves as the canonical place to link from the App Store, website, and press coverage.
- Searchability: people searching for "Notelayer" find an official account, not nothing.

## Account Setup Checklist

- [ ] Claim handle: @Notelayer (check availability; fallbacks: @NotelayerApp, @GetNotelayer).
- [ ] Profile photo: Notelayer app icon (square, 400x400).
- [ ] Header image: app screenshot collage or brand visual (1500x500).
- [ ] Bio: 160 chars max. Should include the tagline and App Store link. Example: "A task manager that pays attention. Track tasks, spot patterns, get smarter over time. Free on iOS → [link]"
- [ ] Website field: link to the marketing landing page (see MARKETING 04).
- [ ] Pin a tweet: announcement or product intro post.

## Content Strategy

### What to Post

- New feature announcements (keep short, show don't tell — screenshot or GIF).
- App Store updates (new version shipped).
- Tips for using Notelayer ("Did you know you can...").
- Replies to productivity / task management conversations where Notelayer is genuinely relevant.
- Retweet positive user feedback or reviews.

### What Not to Post

- Generic productivity quotes.
- Engagement bait ("Like if you make to-do lists!").
- Posting just to maintain a schedule — silence is better than filler.

### Frequency

- At minimum: post for every new app release.
- Ideal: 2–4 posts per week during active growth phase.
- Product tips: 1 per week when there is something genuine to share.

## Cross-Posting from LinkedIn

LinkedIn posts can be adapted for Twitter — strip down to the key point, cut the length to 2–3 short paragraphs (or a thread), add a screenshot.

- Do not copy-paste LinkedIn posts directly — Twitter culture and format are different.
- Threads work well for build-in-public content that is too long for a single tweet.

## Metrics to Track

- Follower count (weekly).
- Impressions per post.
- Link clicks to App Store and website.
- Mentions and replies.
